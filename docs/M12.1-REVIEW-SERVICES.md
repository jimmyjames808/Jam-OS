# M12.1 code check: init, devmgr, the namespace and keep

Part 1b of M12.1 (ROADMAP: "every line M12 changes, with the unchanged
callers of each interface it changes"), the last code check of M12, at
main 452ea7ba, by an agent that wrote none of it. Read in full context
(`git diff 99cfc552 452ea7ba`, 99cfc552 being the commit before M12's
first merge), not only the hunks of S1a, S1b, S2a, S2b, D, P6a, P6b, N3
and the follow-ups:

- **init** (`user/services/init/`): the supervision module's use
  (`spare.c`, `services.c`'s starts and ends, `shell.c`'s loop); P6a's and
  P6b's moves into IDL (initctl's strings in `ctl.c`, `update.offer` in
  `update.c` and `user/lib/updoffer.c`, `splashch` in `splash.c`,
  `crashres` in `lastboot.c` and `user/lib/crashres.c`, the shell's
  `note=`); init's devmgr side (`dm.c`, `services.c`'s devmgr parts);
  the terminals and the desktop (`terms.c`, `comp.c`, `apps.c`); the
  mounts (`mounts.c`) and `main.c`'s test mode;
- **devmgr in IDL** (`user/services/devmgr/`, `abi/idl/devmgr.idl`): the
  addresses (`request.c`'s `find` and `named`), devmgrctl and the
  per-opener channels (`chans.c`), `kill_process`, the PCI disks and
  `disk_storage` (`disks.c`), the nvmetest gate (`main.c`'s match table,
  `bind.c`'s arguments), with `usb.c` and `supervise.c` where they meet;
- **the namespace and keep**: `abi/idl/nschan.idl`, `user/lib/ns.c`;
  `abi/idl/keepch.idl`, `user/lib/keep.c` and the keeper's drain
  (`user/lib/supervise.c`'s `sup_kept_*`);
- **volumes**: its control channels and their hand-written serve loop
  (`user/services/volumes/chans.c`; the rest of volumes was part 1a's);
- the startup roles (`user/include/roles.h`) against every program that
  gives or takes one.

Attacker models: a program the shell starts (its list's services: `svc
net`, `svc audio`, `svc music`, ... are allowed to programs from /data
once the owner allows them, `user/services/shell/sh_allow.c`); a service
compromised through what it parses (netstack and the network, the mixer
and its clients, usb-storage and a stick, a usb-bus and a USB device)
with the channels it holds to init and devmgr; a test program or the
shell holding the trusted channels (`/svc/init`, `/svc/devmgr-ctl`,
`/svc/volumes`); a dead service's queued messages read by its
supervisor.

Severity, as part 1a: **High** lets one ordinary client take a service
away from everyone else (or corrupt what it holds); **Medium** is a
memory-safety slip, data loss on a narrower path, or the same denial with
more effort; **Low** is a narrow case, a stuck state that a restart
clears, a wrong claim or a design point.

**1 High, 0 Medium, 9 Low; 4 design questions.**

What held up:

- **init's calls that don't wait** (D, S2b). `dm.c`'s calls and `ctl.c`'s
  laters each keep a duplicate of the request's channel, match answers by
  txid, time out by a deadline the loop's wait includes, and end with
  ERR_PEER_CLOSED when devmgr or volumes goes (`dm_gone`; volumes': see
  finding 6). A later handed from volumes to devmgr (`later_devmgr_kill`)
  is finished by exactly one path. Handle values can't be reused under a
  stale copy (a 15-bit generation per slot, `kernel/object/handle.c`).
- **initctl's strings** (P6b). Every string is checked by the generated
  dispatch (count, UTF-8, no NUL) and again by the handler for what it
  takes (kill's printable name, mount's `/usbN`, terminal's control
  characters, launch's `[a-z0-9-]`); the buffers are the protocol's cap
  plus the NUL. Who may ask what is the channel's (`struct ctl`), never the
  caller's.
- **update.offer** (P6b): the generated server refuses the form; init
  parses only the manifest, checks the signature before any length or
  hash, copies before it hashes, and the worker's hand-over is release /
  acquire. `crashres` and `splashch` take only well-formed events and make
  the path printable before it reaches the log or the shell's `note=`.
- **devmgr's channels**. Which protocol a channel may speak is decided in
  `chans.c`'s `dispatch` by the channel's level; a device channel answers
  about its own device only (`named`); `get_service` never hands out a
  disk's driver; `disk_storage` keeps a duplicate of the request's channel
  and answers ERR_PEER_CLOSED when the driver goes. Addresses that a kind
  says are 0 are refused (`find`).
- **The nvmetest gate** (4e95e226) holds where it is meant to: without an
  `nvmetest=` word the NVMe function has no driver path, so `rebind`,
  `device_channel`, `disk_storage` and every query answer ERR_NOT_FOUND.
  (`driver_view` and `driver_bar`, the tests' views of a driver's rights,
  reach any PCI function with MSI-X, the NVMe's too: they hand out only a
  driver's rights, and utest's `driver_handle_limits` uses them only for
  refusals. Not a finding.)
- **kill_process and its 15 s** (known item): `kill_request` waits for the
  process for up to STOP_WAIT, but `jam_job_kill` returns only once the
  job is dead, so the wait ends at once; the 15 s only bounds a binding
  with no job. Closed: no wait inside devmgr's loop.
- **The namespace** (P6a). `ns.c` checks every record (a NUL within
  NS_NAME_MAX, a mount or a service name, connect bytes 0 or 1, lengths
  and handles agreeing) before it applies anything, closes every handle it
  doesn't keep, takes at most 64 messages per lookup, and the starter's
  `ns_update` takes back what the program hasn't read. A program's SR_NS
  end can only be read.
- **keep** (P6a). Both sides check what the other sends: the keeper takes
  only channels, VMOs and events it can duplicate, within 256 slots and
  256 handles; a successor checks every restore (slots seen once, counts
  1 to 4, sums, totals in `done`); refused handles are closed. The keeper's
  drain is bounded per turn (1024) and comes back by a packet of its own.
- **The startup roles** (P6b) agree between giver and taker for every
  program but the console's two (finding 7).

## Design questions (reported, not decided)

**A. The unread bound for init's, logd's, devmgr's and volumes' channels
(finding 5).** (a) Give these four jobs message limits (CODING-GUIDE: "every
resource user code can grow is bounded and charged"), and with them the
generated rule (`<proto>_unread_full` before each `_serve_one`, an
`idl_held`, as the compositor's control channels do); (b) leave them: no
limit, no denial, kernel memory bounded per client end. *Recommendation:
(a) at M13's start, when POSIX programs make many more clients; until then
(b) costs nothing anyone can turn against another client.*

**B. init's own asynchronous calls on the ends it publishes (finding 4).**
(a) At each devmgr and volumes start init asks its own end for a channel
of its own (`svc.connect`) and makes its `_send` calls there, so the
published end is never read by init; (b) leave it (holders are the shell
and the tests). *Recommendation: (a), small, with the next init change.*

**C. Hand-written serve loops that serve a channel until it is empty
(finding 3).** *Recommendation: 64 a turn in devmgr's and volumes' `serve`
(the rule part 1a gave volumes' other readers), with the next devmgr or
volumes change.*

**D. The console's control channel and the log writers' table (finding
7).** (a) Remove both (init's `CTL_CONSOLE` slots, `writers.c`, the two
roles, the docs that describe them), keeping the role numbers unused;
(b) give the console a use for them again. *Recommendation: (a): the
compositor has Ctrl+Alt+Del and the notices since Q14.*

## Tests added

- `svc_shared_unread` (finding 1): a child, given `/svc/music`,
  `/svc/audio` and `/svc/notify` by utest, takes the shared ends off its
  own SR_NS (as any program can), writes 16 requests on each with txids of
  its own, and exits without reading; each service must then still open
  (`svc_open`). Run from the shell by `tools/shell-tests/ctl-wire.txt`.
- `devmgr_hostile` (finding 2, and the live devmgr fed garbage): idl_fuzz's
  messages for `devmgr`, `devmgrctl` and `svc` on a query opener (every
  method: the control protocol must be refused there) and on a control
  opener (all but the methods that would stop, rebind, release, kill or
  rewire a real device), each answered within 5 s; devmgr still answers.
- `initctl_hostile` (the live init fed garbage): idl_fuzz's `initctl`
  messages on `/svc/init` (all but reboot, reboot_firmware, kernel_load
  and terminal; the strings' letters can't spell a real process or a
  stick's mount), each answered; init still answers a kill.
