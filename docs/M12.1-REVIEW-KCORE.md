# M12.1 code check: the kernel core

Part 3b of M12.1's check ([ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md), M12.1's row): every
line of the kernel core changed since M8.6 (`git diff 2079f350` at main
ae859bee), read in full context: `kernel/object/` (channels, handles and
`handle_info`, ports, VMOs), `kernel/sched/` (the futex, `thread_yield`,
the direct hand-off), `kernel/mm/`, `kernel/abi/` (every system call's
argument checks), `kernel/arch/` (the FS base, the system call rule for the
FPU, PCID hints), `kernel/proc/`, `kernel/lib/`, `kernel/main.c`, their
headers in `kernel/include/jam/` and `abi/syscalls.def`; about 6,800
lines. Read against M12's intent: [M12-REVIEW-KERNEL.md](M12-REVIEW-KERNEL.md)
and [M12-PLAN.md](M12-PLAN.md)'s answers K-A to K-F, and the format of
[M8.6-CHECK-KCORE.md](history/M8.6-CHECK-KCORE.md). Devices, the IOMMU,
kexec and debug are the kernel-devices reviewer's; what crosses into them
is noted. Findings first; each one's outcome is filled in as it is fixed.

Severity: **High** is a crash, a hole, or a hang or breakage that a
program can cause for others with hostile arguments; **Medium** is wrong
behaviour the system or a test tier will meet; **Low** is a wart, a guide
violation, or a hole only a holder of a power can use on itself.

Before any change, on ae859bee: the full ktest at 4 CPUs on the PC:
418 passed (the RESULTS box's stress line is a test's faked failure, as

## Design questions for the owner

2. Finding 6: should a read-only pin of uncommitted pages commit them?
3. Finding 7: should the reader move when an end's handles drop back to one?
4. Finding 1's rule (only the sole holder marks an end) is the minimal

## Answers (2026-10-09)

- **Finding 1's rule**: kept (only an end's sole holder may mark it). A
  right of its own for the mark adds a right bit for one caller pattern;
  every current setter marks an end it has just made.
- **Finding 6**: the same as KDEV's question A, which the owner answered
  (a read-only pin only on committed pages, the pinner pays); done by the
  KDEV-A track.
- **Finding 7**: left as it is: the accounting goes to the wrong job but
  nothing leaks, and moving the reader on a close needs the handle table
  to report closes of channel ends. Revisit with finding 2.
- **Finding 2** (the owner, 2026-10-09): Q2 changes. A writer's unread
  messages stay charged to the writer's job until they are read or
  dropped, also after the writer closes; a close no longer frees the
  writer's quota, so the backlog can't be passed on and repeated.
  Done (f8c12a2a, 8090336a), with these choices (the safest at each,
  taken without asking):
  - **One job reference per message**, as every queued message already
    had (`chan_msg`'s `job` and `charge`), not a total per end: a queue
    can hold several writers' messages (an end passed on, a forwarder),
    and each read or drop refunds exactly the job that paid. A job with
    no process left is kept by those references alone and destroyed when
    its last message is read or dropped (a lost refund would show in
    every job above it: init's root-job check, the soak's job tree, the
    ktests' `kt_job_is_empty`). No new cycle: a job references only its
    parent.
  - **Nothing is dropped at the writer's close**: what it wrote stays
    readable until `ERR_PEER_CLOSED` (a pipe's end of file, a clipboard
    owner's write-then-close, the compositor's error before it hangs up).
    The reader's close drops its queue and refunds the writers, as it
    always did.
  - **The reader's job is gone whole** (`channel_received`, `reader_job`,
    the handle table's `handle_insert`/`handle_untake` wrappers): nothing
    else used it. `CHANNEL_NO_HANDLES` counts handles
    (`kobject_handles`), `handle_info` sums the queue's bytes; neither
    ever looked at the reader's job. That also ends finding 7 (an end
    held a reference on a spawner's job) and K-A's refinement.
  - Checked and unchanged: `handle_info`'s counts (bytes and messages,
    not charges); the keeper (`keeper_drain` reads a dead service's whole
    keep queue at `keeper_attach`, which is now what releases the dead
    instance's job; init's and devmgr's jobs no longer pay for it);
    kexec (charges nothing to jobs); `JOB_LIMIT_MSG_BYTES`'s readers
    (the shell's `mem`, devmgr's and volumes' child limits, the soak's
    job tree line, userboot's root-job check).
  - **Open for the owner, the same pattern the other way round.** What a
    server writes to a client stays the server's until the client reads
    it or closes, also after the server has hung up. The compositor
    disconnects a client that is too slow or breaks the protocol and
    frees its slot at once; what that client left unread (at most the
    window, 32 batches of up to 16 KiB, and the error) stays charged to
    the compositor while the client keeps its end open. A client that
    gets itself disconnected again and again and keeps every dead end
    pins up to about 512 KiB of the compositor's message bytes per end,
    bounded only by its own handle limit; before M12 the lingering slots
    capped this at 64 connections. The same holds for any server that
    hangs up on a client. Not fixed here (it needs a kernel or protocol
    choice): (a) a close that also drops what the closing end wrote and
    nobody read (the error a client should see goes too); (b) servers
    keep their per-client backlog small before they hang up (the
    compositor could send only the error to a client it disconnects as
    too slow, but the window's batches are already queued); (c) accept
    it, bounded by the compositor's job limit.
  - Tests (each of the four new ktests failed on the old kernel, run on
    the PC: `chan_writer_pays_fill_close_repeat` "job_msg_bytes(f.js) ==
    0 failed: 65392 vs 0", `chan_writer_pays_until_read` and
    `_until_reader_closes` "job_msg_bytes(f.jc) == charged failed: 0 vs
    65392", `chan_writer_pays_after_job_exits` "job_refs(j) == 9 failed:
    1 vs 9"). On the branch, on the PC: the four 5 loops shuffled, idle
    and under load at 8 CPUs, 20 of 20 each; the full ktest at 4 and at 8
    CPUs, 435 passed each; the init run, utest 534 passed, usbtest 15
    passed, the root job clean; the stress run (120 s, 8 CPUs) PASSED;
    the soak at 4 and at 8 CPUs PASS, the job tree's growth +567 pages
    and +4800 message bytes, ending at 162720 message bytes, the same as
    main 1e8cc572's soaks (+567, +4800, 162720). Two test races found on
    the way, both failing on main 1e8cc572 too and fixed in their own
    commits: `channel_call_concurrent` counted a call after its reply
    (3999 vs 4000 under load, 0898c6ab), and utest `jwlc_clip`'s empty
    flood timed the paste from the owner's end arriving instead of from
