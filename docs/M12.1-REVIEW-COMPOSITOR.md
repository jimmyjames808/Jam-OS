# M12.1 review: the compositor

M12.1's part 2a ([ROADMAP](ROADMAP.md)): G1's compositor read whole,
`user/services/compositor/` (about 18,000 lines), at main ae859bee (M12's
P1a and P1b merged: JWL2, flow control by the kernel's count, input as
events). Read as a hostile Wayland client would use it: bad sizes and
offsets in shm buffers, pools resized under buffers, objects destroyed out
of order, ids reused, floods, a client that never reads, absurd surfaces
and limits, a restart with stale state; and as any program that can open
/svc/wayland or /svc/notify. libjwl, the console's window mode, libfun's
Wayland back end and init's wiring are other reviews'.

**What holds up.** The shm path is sound: pools are kept VMOs mapped
`VMAR_KEPT_ONLY`, buffers are checked against the pool with no overflow,
pools only grow, and every read of a client's pixels (paint.c's
`draw_buffer`, `pad_colour`, the cursor) stays inside the buffer it was
checked as. Every per-client object kind is capped, and teardown order
(clipboard, xdg-shell, surfaces, shm, globals, seat) leaves no pointer
into a freed client: `surface_live` guards every event that names a
surface, the seat drops its window pointers in `seat_window_gone` before a
window goes, grabs and the tiler forget a window as it unmaps. Regions
and damage clamp coordinates, so box arithmetic can't overflow. The
state VMO's description is checked whole (counts, indexes, trees, keys,
the checksum) before anything of it is used, and a description that killed
the last compositor isn't tried twice. Configures, acks, roles and popups
follow xdg-shell strictly. Flow control (P1a) holds a client that never
reads to its window and its held bytes.

## Design questions

**DQ1. Who may be a boot overlay.** Today any client that binds no
`wl_keyboard` and asks for full screen is one: over every window and the
strip, on no screen, no chip or Alt+Tab row, never raised under anything,
and while it is up every notice is held. With C3 fixed it can't take keys,
but it can still cover the desktop with whatever it draws (a fake desktop
over a real terminal that keeps the keys) until it is killed blind.
- (A) Only while the boot's wait for the splash is on (`comp.splash_until`,
  init's `splash` argument), and only the first one: later ones are
  ordinary full-screen windows. The splash comes from init at boot only.
- (B) (A), and an overlay's life is bounded (the splash's 5 s and its
  fade): past it, it becomes an ordinary full-screen window.
- (C) A capability: init hands the splash a token on its own connection
  (a compctl `new_client` of an OVERLAY kind), as G2's `xdg-activation`
  will for launches (review W21).

*Recommendation: (A).* One line in `overlay_wanted`, and the splash works
as now; the tests that make overlays (`desk_overlay`, `splash-comp`) start
with the splash's wait.

**DQ2. Caps per program, not per connection.** Every cap is a
connection's; `COMP_CLIENTS_MAX` is everyone's. One program can take all
64 connections (C12) and, with them, 64 times every per-client cap (C13).
The svc protocol's connect doesn't say who asks. (A) Leave it (a program
that does this is visible in `ps` and can be killed); (B) svc.connect
carries the caller's job koid (the kernel's, unforgeable) and the
compositor caps connections per job (say 8).

*Recommendation: (A) until M13* (ported programs), then (B).

**DQ3. Notices pushed out.** A card with buttons pushed out by newer ones
(C11) leaves its poster waiting. (A) Cards with buttons are never pushed
out by cards from another channel (a new one is refused, ERR_NO_RESOURCES,
when 5 are up); (B) the poster's `notify_wait` answers the pushed-out card
with a "dismissed" button number (255).

*Recommendation: (B)*: no poster can block another's notice.

C6 and C7 have no test (the behaviour is the same, or the state can't be
made); C8 removes dead code. C10-C13 are reported only (C11-C13 with the
design questions).

**New hostile tests** (`user/tests/utest/comp_fuzz.c`): `comp_fuzz` (48
seeded rounds of a fuzzer's JWL2 batches against a compositor of the
test's own: real objects and opcodes with arguments chosen to hurt, lying
sizes, noise, handles of every kind; afterwards its handles and message
bytes are back), `comp_live_fuzz` (16 rounds against the desktop's own;
`utest only comp_live` and `g1-hostile.txt` count it) and `comp_chaos`
(the desktop on, three proper clients doing everything careless ones do
and a user's keys and mouse at once, 2,400 seeded steps). They found
nothing more: the compositor stayed up and let go of everything in every
run.
