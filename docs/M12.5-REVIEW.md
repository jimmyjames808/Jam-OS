## The questions, answered first

Each holds unless a finding below says otherwise.

- **A pager that never answers.** Every waiter ends at its request's
  deadline (60 s on every mount), a kill ends a wait at once, and nothing
  waits with a lock held: the region lock is dropped before the wait, and
  PK2's audit still holds (the kernel's mutexes are the region lock,
  `resize`, `setup`, `owner_lock`, VT-d's and kexec's, and none has a copy
  or a pager wait inside). A user fault is killed with the reason, a copy
  returns `ERR_IO`, `vmo_read` `ERR_IO`. A late joiner gets less time
  (finding 9).
- **A pager that answers wrong.** Unaligned or empty ranges are
  `ERR_INVALID_ARGS`; a range past the size fails the whole call (nothing
  published); an unknown key is `ERR_NOT_FOUND`; a fail takes only
  `ERR_IO` or `ERR_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Pages nobody asked for are published
  inside the budget (the pager's own cache), and the bytes are the
  pager's to choose: a wrong offset gives wrong bytes to that file's
  mappers only, and fails every check on a verified VMO. A request only
  partly supplied keeps its waiters until it ends (note N1).
- **A pager that dies mid-fault.** Its requests stay in the object, and
  the successor's `PAGER_NEXT_AGAIN` gives them back; volumes giving up on
  the mount closes the pager (`supervise.c`), which fails every waiter at
  once.
- **Faults racing truncate, close, exit.** A shrink cuts the requests under
  the VMO lock with the size, answers those past the end so their waiters
  look again and meet it (a bus error), and the fault's retry starts from
  `find`; a detach sets `pdetached` (no request made, nothing published)
  and fails the requests; a killed waiter's request stays for the others.
  The close's cost is finding 3.
- **Kernel memory a hostile pager (or client) can pin.** Not bounded by the
  budget: a pager VMO's tables (finding 1, reachable by any program with a
  `/tmp`), a pager's links (finding 2), and a seal's drop (finding 4).
- **The cache.** `resident` counts the listed pages and the reserved
  slots; a full budget hands the oldest page's slot to the new one, which
  is published only after the old one left its table; memory pressure
  evicts the pager's own pages only. As read, the count is right; what it
  leaves out is the tables (finding 1).
- **Verified pages** (PK4, PX):
  - *Read before it's checked?* No. The seal (one hold of the VMO lock,
    no mapping allowed) drops every page present and then sets `verify`;
    from then on `vmo_pager_publish`, the only way a page enters a pager
    VMO's table, takes a page only if it matched: hashed by the supplier
    on its own copy (`checked`), or under the lock for a page copied before
    the seal. `pager_write` refuses a verified VMO; `vmo_write`, commit,
    pins and kernel mappings refuse every pager VMO; a writable pager VMO
    can't be sealed; a copy-on-write child is a VMO of its own without
    `RIGHT_EXEC`.
  - *Swapped after the check?* No: the bytes hashed are the kernel's own
    copy, and that copy is what is published; the pager's buffer is never
    read again.
  - *A different page at a re-fault after eviction?* Checked again
    against the same index's hash: a different page is refused and its
    faulter dies ("a page doesn't match the approved program"); nothing is
    poisoned (ktest `pager_cache_verified_refetch`).
  - *Per page or per file?* The kernel's hashes are per page, bound to
    their index by their place in the table, the last page zero-padded.
    Per file only in user space: `allow` writes the line's whole-file hash
    and the page table from the same sealed bytes (`sh_allow.c`), and a
    run checks the table's header (that hash, the size) against the line
    and the file's size; nothing can check the page hashes against the
    whole-file hash without reading the file. The table is trusted because
    `/data/etc` is outside every program's view, and it comes through the
    same service that supplies the pages (design question 5).
- **fat as the pager, and coherence.** PF2's ordering (flush into the
  hold, then invalidate and set the size, inside the operation, before the
  commit, with fat's one loop serving page requests only between
  operations) holds as read; `grow_to`'s zeros are inside the invalidated
  range. Left: a lost hold (PF2's own "Not done"), the fresh start
  (finding 6), the map slots (finding 5).
- **A pulled stick under a mapping.** volumes' disk-gone path retires the
  mount: fat killed and volumes' handle closed; once both are gone the
  pager's last handle is, so every VMO is detached and every waiter fails
  at once ("the file's pager is gone"); a read fat was doing when the disk went detaches
  its key itself. The cost of that close is finding 3.
- **Plumbing.** `file.map` is method 6; `READ` is the file's one VMO while
  fat holds the file open, `EXEC` a VMO of the caller's own; volumes hands
  the pager with `READ WRITE WAIT` only, to instances and to a spare at
  its promotion, and closes it on retire, give-up and remount (finding 7).

Severity as FR1's: **High** is a loss of data, a crash, a hole, or a hang
a program can cause for others; **Medium** is wrong behaviour the system
or a test tier will meet, or a cost a program can push onto every other
one; **Low** is a wart, or a mismatch nothing relies on yet.

Counts: **1 High, 1 Medium, 8 Low, 4 notes.**

Notes (no change needed now):

- **N1.** A request is answered whole (PK1's rule): a pager that supplies
  only part of a request, or a request's pages in two calls, keeps the
  waiters whose pages arrived waiting until it ends (failed, or its
  deadline). fat and extfs supply each request in one call; `pager.h`
  says another pager must.
- **N2.** `pager_next` fails requests past their deadline only in the
  queue, and a waiter fails its own; a delivered request with no waiter
  left (one a pager drops, or can't copy out: `sysc_pager.c`'s
  `sysc_pager_next`) stays, with its 128 bytes and a reference to its job,
  until `PAGER_NEXT_AGAIN` or the close; a GONE so lost keeps its key
  taken. fat never drops one.
- **N3.** A copy-on-write child of a pager VMO has `related_koid` 0
  (`vmo_pager_koid`) while its faults wait for the pager, so PK1's "how a
  service that maps client VMOs refuses one" doesn't hold through a child.
  No service relies on it: the compositor refuses with `VMAR_KEPT_ONLY`,
  which refuses both.
- **N4.** An evictor pops a page from the list, drops the cache's lock,
  and takes the page out if the table still holds that struct page
  (`vmo_pager_evict`). Were the page freed, reallocated and published at
  the same index of the same VMO between, it would take the new page and
  the count would be one low. Only a pager's own supply publishes, and
  fat and extfs supply from one thread, so it can't happen today; a pager
  that supplied from two threads could meet it.

## Design questions

1. **Who pays for a pager VMO's tables and links (findings 1 and 2).**
   (a) Tables inside the budget, a leaf freed when it empties; (b) links
   and VMO structs charged to the job of whoever makes them (fat's, which
   has limits); (c) limits on volumes' and init's jobs. *Recommendation:*
   (a) and (b); not (c): with both, nothing unbounded is left on those
   jobs, and a limit would make every mount's supplies fail instead of
   the machine. *Taken* (owner, 2026-10-20): (a) and (b), built by PGF,
   with a key limit a pager besides ([PGF notes](M12.5-PLAN.md#pgf-notes)).
2. **One limit for all the caches.** Q3 gave each mount 1/16 of memory,
   and no other program's pressure reclaims it; a drive with 16 FAT
   partitions, sticks and `/tmp` make that more than all of memory.
   *Recommendation:* volumes divides one total (an eighth of memory) among
   its mounts and init's `/tmp`; a kernel-wide limit with the oldest clean
   page of any pager evicted is M14's (global reclaim).
3. **The close's page drop (finding 3).** A kernel thread that drops
   detached VMOs' pages (PK3's suggestion), or `on_zero_handles` and
   `destroy` run with preemption on. *Recommendation:* the reaper thread,
   for pager detaches and for any VMO's destroy over a few MiB; `td_run`'s
   per-CPU list stays as it is. Not built by PGF: not small (its notes
   say why).
4. **A request's deadline (finding 9).** From its making (today), from
   its last join, or from the pager's last progress. *Recommendation:* the
   pager's last progress (a counter bumped by every supply and answer, read
   at the deadline), one rule with WR's for stores (RUNWELL's RW7).
5. **What the kernel's page check guards.** The page table comes from
   `/data/etc/allow.d` through the same service that supplies the
   program's pages, and the allow line too. The check stops a file changed
   after its approval (by a program that can write it, or on another
   computer) and any page changed between check and use; it does not stop
   a compromised `/data` service, which can serve a table to match its
   pages. PX's reason for checking in the kernel ("a fat broken by a
   crafted filesystem could then serve any code") holds for sticks, whose
   services never serve programs, not for `/data`'s own. *Recommendation:*
   say so where PJ writes ARCHITECTURE's paragraph; if a compromised
   `/data` service belongs in the model, the approvals need a key it
   doesn't have (a MAC written by `allow` with a key held by init and the
   shell, never on `/data`): M14's to weigh.
6. **A remount and its mappings (finding 7).** Keep a mount's pager and
