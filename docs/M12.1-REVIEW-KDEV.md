# M12.1 code check: the kernel's devices

Part 3a of M12.1 (ROADMAP: "the kernel's changes since M8.6 ... a kernel
bug is a crash or a hole"), at main ae859bee, by an agent that wrote none
of it. Read in full context, not only the hunks of
`git diff 2079f350 HEAD`:

- `kernel/dev/` (the VT-d units, queue, faults, domains, page tables,
  interrupt remapping, shared requester ids, the report; `random.c`,
  `reboot.c`, `wallclock.c`) and `kernel/acpi/dmar.c`;
- `kernel/object/dma_cap.c` and `vmo.c`'s pin and quarantine paths, with
  `kernel/abi/sysc_hw.c`'s pin calls (the brief names "DMA caps and
  quarantine"; the files are the kernel-core reviewer's directory, so only
  the pin paths were read here, and the one edit there is named below);
- `kernel/kexec/` (`jump.c`, `load.c`; the rest is unchanged since M8.6);
- `kernel/debug/` (the panic screen, its drawing and codes, `panic.c`,
  the lock checker, the debug commands, the stress run);
- their headers in `kernel/include/jam/` and their ktests.

Against CODING-GUIDE.md, ARCHITECTURE.md ("The IOMMU", "Kexec: reboot and
panic"), docs/history/M11-REVIEW.md (whose findings and answers are not
repeated) and M12-REVIEW-KERNEL.md (K2's findings 10-15). Attacker models:
a driver process (its handles, its `dma_cap`, its device's every DMA and
interrupt write), a hostile user program (the system calls that reach
these files: `random_get`, `vmo_pin` and `vmo_unpin`, `dma_vmo_create`,
`kexec_load`, the debug commands), and the device racing the kernel.

Severity, as M11's review: **High** lets a device reach memory its driver
didn't pin (or a page after its unpin or its cap's close, before the unit
confirmed), raise an interrupt it wasn't given, or a program crash or hang
the kernel; **Medium** is the same on a narrower path, or a broken rule
that can open such a hole; **Low** is a narrow case, a wrong claim or a
design point.

**Nothing High was found** (2 Medium, 7 Low). What held up (beyond M11's
list, which still holds):

- **K2's pins.** A read-only pin is read only where the IOMMU makes it so:
  `sysc_hw.c` asks RIGHT_READ only when the cap has a domain, `vmo.c` maps
  with `iommu_map_ro` from the same fixed `c->dom`, and the range is a
  "writer" (refused on a sealed VMO, blocking a seal) exactly when the
  device may write. One permission per page and domain (`vtd_pt.c`
  `map_one`), so a read-only pin is never writable because a writable pin
  of the page exists, and a writable pin is refused, not silently read
  only. The unpin checks every page's permission before changing any, and
  any unmap error keeps the pages (`pin_keep`). The pin/close race
  (`closed` checked under the cap's lock before publishing; the close
  takes only non-busy pins; the release thread waits for pins in flight
  before destroying the domain) is unchanged and right.
- **The job pays.** `dma_cap_create` needs RIGHT_MANAGE on the job it
  charges; the cap's handle unit and every table page (`vtd_pt.c`
  `table_new`) are charged to it before they exist and uncharged when they
  go; a kept domain (unconfirmed) keeps its charge and its job reference.
- **Bounds.** Every user length is checked before any allocation or
  multiplication (`sysc_vmo_pin`: 4096 pages, 32 KiB of addresses;
  `random_get`: 256 bytes on the stack; `kexec_load`'s command line). The
  panic screen's drawing clips every pixel (`put`), whatever the
  framebuffer's size; its panel copes with a screen narrower than its
  labels.
- **Off paths.** `iommu_jump_off` runs after bus mastering is off on every
  path that leaves the kernel (the kexec jump, the panic jump, the firmware
  reset) and takes no lock; a failed jump turns into the panic screen's
  second case and its reset, never a dark hang.
- **The lock checker's fast path** (push: depth then slot; pop: slot then
  depth; a release checked exactly when its acquisition was) is safe
  against an interrupt handler at any step.

### Design questions for the owner

- **A (finding 4).** Charge a pin's handle unit to the pinner (the cap's
  job, the driver's) rather than the VMO's holder, and/or let a read-only
  pin take only pages already committed (`ERR_BAD_STATE` otherwise)?
  Recommendation: both; the drivers' read-only pins (transmit rings, the
  sound ring) are of buffers they made and committed themselves.
- **B (finding 2).** Peer-to-peer and ACS: (a) refuse, (b) enable, or both
  (recommended: (b) then (a)). Only matters on hardware with a PCIe switch
  or a multi-function endpoint; the PC has neither in a driver's path.
- **C (finding 3, decided here, easy to undo).** `reset=none` no longer
  survives a reboot (the other reset words do). If it should, revert the
  commit.

## Hostile and fuzz tests added

Where reading found the tests thinnest: the pin paths driven the way a
driver can drive them, and the device against an unmap (4d9ec2a4; all pass
with and without the IOMMU, and found nothing new):

- `dma_pin_fuzz` (new `kernel/test/test_dma_fuzz.c`): 600 random steps a
  seed through the system call layer (`sys_vmo_pin_flags`,
  `sys_vmo_unpin`, `sys_vmo_decommit`, the cap's last handle closed with
  pins held and a new cap made) on three VMOs with a writable and a
  read-only handle each: bad offsets, lengths and flags, read-only pins
  through either handle, pins overlapping others both ways, unpins of
  live, dead, made-up and other VMOs' ids. After every step the result is
  the model's, the cap's pin count is the model's, a pinned page can't be
  decommitted, and with the IOMMU every page's leaf in the domain has the
  model's pin count and permission (absent when unpinned). Four fixed seeds
  and one from the clock, printed.
- `dma_pin_races_close`: a thread pins and unpins while the cap's last
  handle closes, 24 rounds at varied moments: nothing left on the cap or in
  the quarantine, the function's context entry off the dead domain every
  round, no page or table leaked.
- `dma_iommu_unpin_races_device`: edu's queued write (it runs ~100 ms
  after the command) against the unpin at 13 delays from 0 to 160 ms: about
  half land before the unpin returns, the rest are blocked, and no page
  changes once the unpin has returned.

`tools/vtd-test.sh`'s DMA boots look for all four new tests.
