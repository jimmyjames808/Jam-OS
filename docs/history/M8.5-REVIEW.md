# M8.5 (kexec for reboot and panic) review

An independent read of what M8.5 added, at commit 2bb820e: `kernel/kexec/`,
the handoff and its check (`kernel/boot/kexec.c`,
`<jam/kexec_handoff.h>`), the panic path's part (`kernel/debug/panic.c`,
the NMI halt in `kernel/arch/x86_64/ipi.c`, bus mastering off in
`kernel/dev/pci.c`), the next boot's reading of the crash record, the
kernel's own AP startup (`apboot.c`, `trampoline.S`, `smp.c`'s claim and
numbering, `cpu.c`'s MTRR and firmware checks, the LAPIC's INIT and SIPI),
init's reboot and last-boot paths, logd's save of the dead boot's log, the
console's blank, the shell's `reboot`, and `tools/kdump-test.sh` and
`tools/kexec-reboot-test.sh`. Findings first; each one's outcome is filled
in as it is fixed.

Severity: **High** loses the panicked boot's log or breaks one of the plan's
binding rules in a way the system will meet; **Medium** is wrong behaviour
a user will meet; **Low** is a wart, a rare race, or a note.

Before any change, on 2bb820e: `tools/kdump-test.sh` and
`tools/kexec-reboot-test.sh` both PASS at 4 CPUs.

## Findings

| # | Sev | Where | What |

| 1 | High | `kernel/kexec/crashlog.c:73-95,127-142`, `kernel/kexec/jump.c:139-147` | The crash record is all or nothing. The next boot sets `after_panic` (the crash-loop rule) only if every field passes: a panic message with one byte outside printable ASCII, or a log ring over 1 MiB (`RING_MAX`), and the record is "no use". The log is lost **and the crash-loop rule is off**, so a panic that comes back at boot restarts the machine forever. The writer copies the message as it is (only `\n` is replaced): user-chosen thread and process names reach panic messages (`uentry.c:129`), and track A of M8.6 makes the ring 1-4 MiB, which would turn the rule off for every panic. |
| 2 | Medium | `kernel/kexec/jump.c:202-213` (kexec_halted_wait), `kernel/debug/panic.c:126-127` | A jump that is decided but never made hangs the machine with a dark screen, for good. And a second fault on the panicking CPU after the decision (its thread name, a corrupt `current`, the backtrace, bus mastering off) ends in `halt_forever` with the screen already dark: no panic screen, no restart. A panic caused by memory corruption is the likely one to fault again. |

Not findings (checked and correct):
- The rules of the plan: the region is out of the HHDM and every kernel
  table (ktest `kexec_region_unmapped`), written only through the window;
  the panic path's checksum reads go through the window's own entries
  with INVLPG (global entries, so any PCID); the panic path takes only
  locks it has force-unlocked (klog, fbcon, serial) and allocates nothing;
  the next kernel resets devices with its normal drivers, and its LAPIC
  init EOIs any vector left in service; nothing uses RAM across a firmware
  reset (the record reaches the next kernel only through the handoff).
- The trampoline (`tramp.S`): CR3 holds PCID 0 before CR4.PCIDE is
  cleared (else #GP), clearing PGE and PCIDE drops every TLB entry, the
  page is mapped at T in both tables, the stack is 16-byte aligned with a
  0 return address as Limine's.
- The AP trampoline on real hardware, beyond the accessed bits: the GDT's
  descriptors (L with D clear for 64-bit code), the far pointers'
  operand sizes in 16- and 32-bit code, PAE before LME before PG, an
  identity-mapped page, CR3 below 4 GiB, NXE set before the kernel half's
  NX leaves are walked, nothing loaded through the trampoline GDT after
  the switch to the kernel's CR3 until `percpu_load`'s `lgdt`, INIT and
  SIPI encodings (level assert; the trigger bit ignored since the
  Pentium 4), x2APIC and xAPIC ICR writes with the delivery-status wait
  bounded.
- The claim: one compare-and-swap decides between the AP and the BSP; an
  abandoned CPU is sent INIT and its memory is freed only after the INIT
  delay; renumbering happens before any AP uses its index.
- The handoff and the record are wire formats with explicit numbers, no
  padding (static asserts), magic, version, size and checksum; strings are
  checked for their terminator before use.
- `kexec_load`: every check comes before the region is written, the ELF's
  plan is copied out before use (no double fetch of offsets), segments
  are ordered and inside the kernel range, so no write leaves the region.

## Outcomes

(Filled in as each finding is fixed.)
- **1 (High): fixed.** The record is checked in two steps
  (`crashlog.c`): the record itself (place, magic, version, size,
  checksum, kind), which alone decides whether the previous kernel
  panicked, and then a panic's log, whose failure only loses the log. A
  name that is not one is dropped (logd numbers the file), the message is
  cleaned instead of refused, and `RING_MAX` is 16 MiB with a static
  assert that this kernel's own ring passes. The writer cleans the message
  too (`kexec_message_clean`: a newline as a space, anything else outside
  printable ASCII as `?`). Tests: ktest `kexec_crash_record_counts_any_panic`
  (a UTF-8 message, a 2 MiB ring, a ring too big or out of place, a bad
  name, a reboot, a damaged record; it failed on the old check at the
  UTF-8 message) and `kexec_message_clean`.
- **2 (Medium): fixed.** The BSP waits for the hand-over at most 10 s
  (`HANDOVER_WAIT_S`), and a nested panic on the CPU that decided to jump
  (identified by its APIC id from CPUID, which needs no per-CPU state)
  calls `kexec_panic_failed`; both force the log's, the screen's and the
  serial port's locks open, log one line and reset through the firmware.
  A nested panic while the red screen is drawn, or on another CPU, halts
  as before. Tests: crash tests `kexecstall` (cpu 1 panics and never
  hands the jump over) and `kexecfault` (a page fault after the decision),
  in `tools/crash-test.sh`: both reset in QEMU (the stall after 10 s);
  with the fix taken out both runs hang until the script's timeout.
- **4: done.** `KEXEC_DEFAULT_MIB` is 32 (the stored kernel takes 12464
  KiB in QEMU); `crashkernel=` still overrides it (32..1024, 0: off).
  New ktest `kexec_region_has_room`: the stored kernel takes at most three
  quarters of the region, so growth shows in QEMU before it stops fitting
  on the PC. Checked in QEMU: a reboot, a second reboot and then a panic
  in one run, each next kernel finding its 32 MiB (the regions move
  between generations, as the previous kernel's image becomes free RAM),
  and the kexec ktests passing live in the fourth kernel.
- **5: done.** The entry is gone from `boot/limine.conf`; the boot word
  `smp=loader` stays, typed into an entry with Limine's editor (E, then
  F10), as HARDWARE.md and TESTING.md now say. No test needed beyond the
  image's: a menu entry; checked that the built image's limine.conf has no
  such entry and that `smp=loader` still releases the CPUs through Limine
  (`ktest=smp smp=loader`, 4 CPUs).
- **10 (Low): fixed.** Once a logd has been given the log, the wait is
  `SAVE_WAIT` and the line says logd didn't finish, even if that logd
  died and the next one was not started yet. No test: it needs a logd
  that dies after 20 s without answering; `tools/kdump-test.sh` `nostick`
  and `save` still pass (the two lines it can show).
