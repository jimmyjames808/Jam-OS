# M8.6 code check: the kernel core

The kernel-core part of M8.6's final code check, at main febd124:
`kernel/mm`, `kernel/sched`, `kernel/object`, `kernel/arch`, `kernel/proc`,
`kernel/lib`, their headers in `kernel/include/jam` and their tests, read
against CODING-GUIDE.md and ARCHITECTURE.md. Findings first; each one's
outcome is filled in as it is fixed.

Severity: **High** hangs or breaks something a program can reach with
hostile arguments; **Medium** is wrong behaviour the system or a test tier
will meet; **Low** is a guide violation or a wart.

Before any change, on febd124: `ktest keep` at 1 CPU: 247 passed, 1 FAILED
(vmo_concurrent_decommit); `ktest=m4_milestone loops=40 keep` at 1 CPU: 39
passed, 1 FAILED.

Not findings (checked and correct):
- Handle tables: generation and FIFO reuse, in-transit reservations
  (take/untake/commit/reserve) and their job charges; table teardown is
  never concurrent with an insert (process_start holds `setup`).
- Channels: the pair lock / endpoint lock order, calls answered by txid,
  close and destroy, the writable signal recomputed under the pair lock.
- Ports, interrupts, timers, events, VMARs, resources, DMA caps and the
  quarantine: lock orders as documented, every user-growable list capped
  and charged.
- VMOs and address spaces: bounds written so they can't wrap, the reverse
  map against decommit and shrink, the TLB gather (pages freed only after
  the shootdown), the PCID generations, table charges, splits.
- The scheduler's on_cpu handshake, wake races (the task_rq_lock loop and
  the BLOCKED -> RUNNING CAS), cancellation, sleeper queues, mutex hand-off.
- The physical allocator's stashes and the heap's magazines; the vmap
  area's guard pages and reuse.
- Entry code: SFMASK, CLAC on every entry, sysret's RIP and RFLAGS
  checks, swapgs windows; user copies with exception fixups.
- The ELF loader and bootfs validation treat every field as hostile.

## Tests after the fixes

On the branch's tip: `make`, `make KTESTS=0`, `make check` clean; ktest
251 passed at 1, 4 and 8 CPUs (at 8 the boot's timer self-check said
MISMATCH, 82 ticks in a second: the Mac's load average was 20-35 with the
other agents' QEMU runs); the `init` run clean (utest 105, usbtest 12/10
skipped, root job clean); the stress run (8 CPUs, 120 s) PASSED;
`ktest loops=5 seed=1004 keep load` at 4 CPUs: 1138 passed, 2 FAILED
(finding 15, fixed after it). `tools/soak-test.sh` at 4 CPUs FAILS at its
first step on this branch and on main febd124 alike: `mount -w /usb0` says
"not mounted" right after init logged `/usb0 mounted` (the shell's view of
the mounts, user space), so the soak never reaches its kernel loops.

## For other tracks and the owner

- `RIGHT_RESIZE` is in: usb-storage can now map a writable channel's
  buffer (its handle to the client already lacks the right), and fat
  could map its transfer buffers instead of bouncing; fat's header
  (`user/services/fat/fileops.c`) and usb-storage's (`drivers/usb-storage/block.c`)
  still say a client with RIGHT_WRITE can shrink the buffer.
- The soak's `mount -w /usb0` failure above (main too): user space's.
- Design, not decided: vmo.c, sched.c and aspace.c over 800 lines (split
  vmo.c's pins and quarantine off?); the job tree walks could become
  iterative; send_icr's xAPIC wait could get a deadline.
